Background and Recognition

I am a Professor at the Lunar and Planetary Laboratory (LPL) and Department of Geosciences at the University of Arizona.

My research as a volcanologist combines field observations, remote sensing, and mission development through planetary analog research, including studies of the 2014–2015 Holuhraun and 2021–2023 Fagradalsfjall eruptions in Iceland.

My work has been featured by CNN, BBC, CBS 60 Minutes, Channel 4, Discovery Channel, and Netflix documentaries.

Ph.D. Geology and Geophysics, University of Hawai'i at Mānoa

Opportunities

I welcome inquiries from motivated individuals interested in planetary volcanism, astrobiology, and exploration technology.

There are no project-specific vacancies at this time, but I am always open to hearing from potential collaborators at all career stages. The best first step is to send me an email at chamilton@arizona.edu describing your background and interests.

Above all, I am looking for three qualities: Creativity, Technical Capability, and Independence.

Take a course with me.

Undergraduate Students

I support undergraduate research through several pathways: independent study for course credit, paid internships through the Arizona Space Grant program, and Honors thesis advising through the Geosciences, Planetary Science, and Astronomy programs.

I am open to working with students from a wide range of disciplines—from Art to Architecture—so reach out.

Graduate Students

Prospective graduate students should contact me before applying to discuss research interests and fit. Formal applications are submitted through LPL Graduate Admissions.

Our primary research areas are:

Geologic Surface Processes—lava emplacement, magma–water interactions, volcano-tectonics, aeolian processes, megaflooding, impact cratering
Astrobiology—microbial colonization of volcanic terrains, biosignature detection, habitability
Geoinformatics—remote sensing, information theory, Bayesian inference, clustering analysis, self-organization, digital twinning, machine learning, and computer vision

I expect applicants to have a strong foundation in one or more of: Physics, Chemistry, Geology, Biology, or Informatics, with technical background in Mathematics, Natural Sciences, and/or Information Technology.

Postdoctoral Researchers

Postdoctoral opportunities follow the same research areas and skill expectations as graduate positions, with additional emphasis on advanced geophysical and computational methods, numerical modeling capabilities, and information technology skills. I encourage prospective postdoctoral researchers to reach out about fellowship opportunities and potential collaborations.
